# Comparison with other networks

How recent is public data on other intelligence community infrastructure, compared with the NSA's own networks?

The picture elsewhere is somewhat fresher, mostly through procurement and modernisation news rather than leaks:

| Network | What is public | Source | Date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| JWICS (DIA's TS/SCI network) | Users put at roughly 200,000, in a report on DIA awarding a modernisation contract | C4ISRNET | December 2022 |
| STONEGHOST (DIA's Five Eyes TS/SCI intel-sharing network) | Planned to expand to more coalition nations by 2024 | Defense News | March 2023 |
| INDOPACOM Mission Network | A 2023-initiated network with about 29,000 users, billed as the first with a native zero-trust architecture | Breaking Defense | June 2025 |
| SIPRNet and NIPRNet | User counts (roughly 500,000 and 4,000,000) and architecture | Open DoD and press material; DISA publishes contract and modernisation notices regularly | Ongoing |
| White House TNet and NICE | Described in a report | New York Times | 2019 |
| **NSA-specific networks** (NSANet, TAONet, Bullrun community of interest) | Essentially nothing public newer than the Snowden corpus and the 2007 domain change, apart from job postings that name NSANet in passing | See [provenance.md](provenance.md) | 2007 to 2013 |

JWICS is the closest analogue to NSANet and has had more recent public coverage because it is a contracted, multi-agency system.

## The pattern

DoD-run and multi-agency networks leave a paper trail through contracts, Congressional briefings and modernisation announcements. NSA's own networks do not, so the public record there is frozen around 2007–2013 and only resurfaces when something breaks.

The November 2026 outage is the latest such moment: see the [timeline](timeline.md). Whether NSANet has been re-architected since 2007 is an [open question](open-questions.md#unanswered).
