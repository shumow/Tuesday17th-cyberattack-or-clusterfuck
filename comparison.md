How recent is public data on other intelligence community infrastructure, compared with the NSA's own networks? As of 17 November 2026.

The picture elsewhere is somewhat fresher, mostly through procurement and modernisation news rather than leaks:

| Network | What is public | Source | Date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| JWICS (DIA's TS/SCI network) | Users put at more than 200,000 by DIA's chief information officer, in a report on DIA awarding a modernisation contract | C4ISRNET | December 2022 |
| STONEGHOST (DIA's Five Eyes TS/SCI intel-sharing network) | Design work on an upgrade was to start in fiscal 2024, to allow sharing with more countries | Defense News, carrying a C4ISRNET story | March 2023 |
| INDOPACOM Mission Network | A network with 29,000 users, billed as the first and largest data-centric zero-trust system authorised for operational use. No start date is given | Breaking Defense, in a sponsored post by the contractor that runs the network | June 2025 |
| SIPRNet and NIPRNet | User counts (roughly 500,000 and 4,000,000) and architecture; DISA publishes contract and modernisation notices regularly | The Electrospaces table: the SIPRNet figure footnoted to Popular Mechanics, the NIPRNet figure with no footnote. No DoD or press page giving both was found | Not dated |
| White House TNet and NICE | Described in an explainer | New York Times, read in a republication | 2019 |
| **NSA-specific networks** (NSANet, TAONet, Bullrun community of interest) | Essentially nothing public newer than the Snowden corpus and the 2007 domain change, apart from job postings that name NSANet in passing | No source named; the NSANet claims are in [provenance.md](provenance.md) | Frozen around 2007–2013 |

All five lines match rows of the [Electrospaces](sources.md#electrospacesnet) table and its footnote links, so this comparison leans on the same blog as the NSANet detail.

JWICS is the closest analogue to NSANet and has had more recent public coverage because it is a contracted, multi-agency system.

## The pattern

DoD-run and multi-agency networks leave a paper trail through contracts, Congressional briefings and modernisation announcements. NSA's own networks do not, so the public record there is frozen around 2007–2013 and only resurfaces when something breaks.

The November 2026 outage is such a moment: see the [timeline](timeline.md). Whether NSANet has been re-architected since 2007 is an [open question](open-questions.md#unanswered).
