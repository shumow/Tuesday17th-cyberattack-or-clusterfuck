## 17 November 2026

**NSANet reported down; later the same day the NSA called it scheduled maintenance.**

Later on 17 November, after the first reports, the picture changed. This block was added on a later pass that opened the sources; see [source-check.md](source-check.md).

- NSA statement, carried by The Record, ABC News 4 and Ynetnews (the last crediting Reuters): the network had a temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance, some internal systems were affected, and mission-critical capabilities stayed operational, on infrastructure built with layers of redundancy. The NSA's own press release was not opened.
- The Record, from its own sources, said the network was not completely shut down but giving intermittent service.
- The later CNN wire version and Ynetnews said intelligence sharing with the Five Eyes partners was not affected; the later CNN version added a White House spokesperson saying there was no evidence of a cyberattack.

Source type: an on-record agency statement, relayed by the outlets above. It is the first official account and firmer than the anonymous-source reporting below. The reading rule applies to it too.

The CNN report:

- CNN reported, citing six sources, that the network "called NSA Net" which "allows employees to communicate classified information internally" had gone down, with no evidence of a cyberattack at that point and staggered measures enacted while scope was assessed.
- Forbes, The Record, ABC and others repeated the same framing.

Later reporting:

- Wall Street Journal (via Jerusalem Post): a person familiar said the NSA was "simply updating its software to patch the system," that NSANet would only be temporarily offline, and there was no evidence of a cyberattack.
- Nextgov/FCW: six people familiar said the outage slowed analysts' workflows and forced staggered arrivals; four of them said it may be linked to a series of software upgrades on the network. Nextgov describes NSA Net as "the primary platform for transmitting top-secret data."
- New York Times (syndicated): one person said NSANet had been unstable for weeks, fuelling frustration in the workforce.

Source type: anonymous-source journalism throughout. These reports confirm the network's continued existence and its role as the internal classified communications platform, but give no technical detail. Who sourced what is in [sources.md](sources.md#november-2026-reporting). Publication dates for the later reports are not recorded; all had appeared by 17 November 2026.

What it adds up to: [cyberattack versus self-inflicted](assessment.md#cyberattack-versus-self-inflicted).

## 13 November 2026

**Other outages before the NSANet outage (context).**

The NSANet outage followed a Microsoft Outlook/Teams outage on 13 November, an FAA ground stop and online banking outages; Microsoft and the FAA each said they found no sign of malicious activity. No source has linked these events.

Source type: not recorded. No source is named for this item yet, and the FAA ground stop and the banking outages are undated. Both are tasks in [open-questions.md](open-questions.md#needs-sourcing).

## 2013

**Prior outage: the NSA's public website went dark.**

The Record cites a 2013 incident in which the NSA's public website went dark; officials attributed it to a scheduled update, which is unrelated to the classified network.

Source: The Record. No source type is recorded for this item: a task in [open-questions.md](open-questions.md#needs-sourcing). It is not in the [provenance table](provenance.md#claim-by-claim).

## January 2000

**Prior outage: a crash of nearly three days at Fort Meade.**

CNN's 17 November 2026 report notes that in 2000 a technical failure at the agency downed computers for nearly three days and interrupted intelligence processing at Fort Meade, per an agency spokesperson at the time. That January 2000 crash was widely reported but not publicly tied to NSANet by name.

Source type for CNN's account: journalism citing an on-record official statement. This is the one on-record item in the [provenance table](provenance.md#claim-by-claim). The statement that the crash was widely reported names no source yet: a task in [open-questions.md](open-questions.md#needs-sourcing).
