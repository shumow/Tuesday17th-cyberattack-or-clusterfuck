## Reading rule

> Don't trust the denial, don't trust the rumour. We're all going to be very calm and critical of the information that we're getting.

In a crisis the first casualty is truth, and that applies equally to official reassurance and to the scarier story that fills the gap it leaves.

## NSANet: What Is Publicly Known and Its Provenance

Working notes compiled 17 November 2026, prompted by reports that NSA's classified internal network went down. The question: is NSANet a real, documented thing, what do open sources actually say about it, how old and how good is that evidence, and how does it compare with what is publicly known about other parts of the US intelligence community's infrastructure?

### Summary

NSANet is real and has been publicly documented since at least the late 1990s. The strongest evidence is primary: leaked NSA internal newsletters (SIDtoday, 2003–2007) that refer to NSANet as the agency's "home" system and carry a standing notice that articles may not be reposted outside NSANet. Secondary and tertiary sources (the Electrospaces blog, Wikipedia) add detail on classification level, colour coding, users, management and hosted tools, mostly derived from Snowden-era documents, FOIA releases and job postings. The weakness is recency: nearly all substantive public detail is 2003–2015 vintage. The only current confirmation is the November 2026 outage reporting, which describes the network functionally but not technically.

## How updates work

- The status table above always shows the latest dated update.
- New reporting goes into [timeline.md](timeline.md) as a dated entry, newest first.
- Every claim keeps its source, source type and date. A claim without a source goes into [open-questions.md](open-questions.md) until it has one.
- A new outlet also goes into the Sources list at the end of [timeline.md](timeline.md#sources) and into [sources.md](sources.md).
- If the current best explanation changes, [assessment.md](assessment.md) changes with it, including the date in its first line.
- A page that carries an "as of" date gets a new date whenever that page changes.
